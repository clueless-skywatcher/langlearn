Nouns I - Gender

1. Nouns are what designate stuff like people, creatures, places or qualities. As mentioned in a previous section, nouns always start with a capital letter. E.g. Apfel, Buch, Katze.

2. Every noun is assigned one of three genders: masculine (das Maskulinum), feminine (das Femininum) and neuter (das Neutrum). Learners must memorize the gender along with the noun since the nouns don't follow any natural gender rules, and inanimate objects can also have a non-neuter gender. E.g. der Mann and der Apfel is masc, die Frau and die Tasche is fem, das Kind and das Essen are neuter.

3. If nouns are written in plural, they always take the feminine article "die", no matter their gender. E.g. der Apfel -> die Aepfel, die Katze -> die Katzen, das Kind -> die Kinder.

4. There are classes of nouns that usually follow a gender. For masculine:

4.1 Male humans and animals E.g. der Hund, der Mann, der Arzt, der Kater, der Vater.

4.2 Months and days of the week. E.g. der Montag, der Februar

4.3 Directions E.g. der Norden, der Suden

5. These classes of nouns are usually feminine

5.1 Female beings E.g. die Frau, die Katze, die Mutter

5.2 Vehicle names [Add some well-known airplane/ship names]

5.3 German nouns that are numbers themselves E.g. die Eins, die Tausend

6. These classes of nouns are usually neuter

6.1 Younglings E.g Maedchen (Even if it seems to be feminine), das Kind, das Baby

6.2 Letters of the alphabet (Swiss use masculine) E.g. das A, das B

6.3 Hotels, cafes, restaurants, cinemas E.g. das Taj, das Hilton, das Airbnb

7. In case of a lot of masculine nouns, one can add an "-in" to the end to make it feminine. E.g. der Arzt -> die Aerztin, der Schauspieler - die Schauspielerin, der Koch -> die Koechin

8. If a word ends with "-mann" it is masculine. Replace "-mann" with "-frau" to make it feminine. E.g. der Kaufmann -> die Kauffrau, der Zimmermann -> die Zimmerfrau
