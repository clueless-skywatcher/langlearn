German Sounds, Spelling and Capitalization
1. Has standard Latin alphabet, with umlaut-ed letters (A, O, U) and an "double-S" letter called eszett that looks like Greek "beta".
2. Letter A-umlaut [for notes, writing it as ae, don't write that into the content] is pronounced like "a" in "fade" or "head". E.g: Aepfel, Maedchen, Naehe.
3. If an "r" is after A-umlaut, it is often pronounced as "air". E.g Aerztin, Maerz.
4. The O-umlaut (oe) is somewhat pronounced somewhere between "e" and "o" [Correct this fact from online sources, maybe I'm wrong somewhere]. E.g. schoenen, froehlich, oeffnen.
5. The U-umlaut (ue) is somewhat pronounced between "u" and "e" [Again, feel free to correct this]. E.g. Mueller, schluessel
6. The eszett (ss) is a simple sharp S. Eszett is never seen at the beginning of a word. E.g. Strasse, Fuss, Spass.
7. Like English, the first word of a german sentence always has a capital letter, no matter its form. E.g. Mein Name ist Hans. Dein Buch ist interessant.
8. Nouns always, and without exception, begin with a capital letter. If a word is in the middle of a sentence and does not have capitals, it is not a noun. E.g. Der Mann liest ein Buch. Ich mag die Katzen.
9. The German "ch" sound is pronounced differently, depending on what precedes it. If "ch" is preceded by a front vowel or a consonant, it is pronounced close to a "sh". This is called an Ich-Laut. E.g. mich, Licht, ich
10. If "ch" is preceded by a back vowel, it is pronounced more like a "kh". This is called Ach-Laut. E.g. Buch, Doch, Achtung.
11. The diphthong "eu" is not pronounced like "ew", but more like "oi" in "oil" or "oy" in "boy". E.g. Euro, Leute
